# Lesson 5: Freelance and side income (provisional tax)

## By the end, the learner can

- Say whether income from freelancing, rent or a side business makes them a provisional taxpayer.
- Explain why provisional tax exists and when the payments are due (`facts.md`).
- Set money aside for tax from every payment they receive.
- Name the business expenses they may be able to deduct, and why receipts matter.

## Key ideas

**Why provisional tax exists.** Nobody deducts PAYE from freelance or rental income. Provisional
tax is how you pay that tax during the year instead of in one painful lump.

**Who is a provisional taxpayer.** Broadly, anyone who earns income that isn't a salary, such as
freelance fees, rent or business profit. There are exemptions for small amounts; check SARS's
provisional tax page for the current rules.

**How it works.** You estimate your taxable income for the year and pay half the tax by the end of
August and the rest by the end of February (form IRP6). An optional third top-up payment after
the year reduces interest if you underestimated. Dates are in `facts.md`.

**Underestimating costs money.** SARS can charge penalties and interest if your estimate is too
low or you pay late.

**Expenses.** Costs you incur to earn freelance income (equipment, software, a share of home office
costs if you meet the rules) can reduce taxable income. Keep receipts and an invoice record.

**The habit that saves you:** move a fixed share of every payment into a separate account the day
it arrives. Use the brackets in `facts.md` to pick a percentage that matches your top bracket.

## Exercise

Ask the learner to work out what share of each R10 000 freelance payment to set aside if their
total income puts them in the 31% bracket, and which dates go in their calendar.
