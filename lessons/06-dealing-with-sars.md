# Lesson 6: Dealing with SARS, and spotting scams

## By the end, the learner can

- Spot a fake SARS email, SMS or call.
- Say what to do if they owe SARS money they can't pay.
- Say what to do if they disagree with an assessment.

## Key ideas

**Scams are common.** SARS never asks for passwords, PINs or banking details by email, SMS or
phone, and never sends links to "claim your refund". Refunds only go to the bank account on your
eFiling profile. When in doubt, don't click: log in to eFiling yourself and check there.

**If you owe and can't pay,** don't ignore it. Interest and penalties grow. SARS can arrange a
payment plan (a deferral) if you ask before it takes collection steps.

**If you disagree with an assessment,** you can ask for reasons, then lodge a formal objection
on eFiling within the time limit on your assessment. Collect your evidence first.

**Changing banks or address:** update your details on eFiling; SARS may ask you to verify in
person or by video for bank changes, to stop fraud.

## Exercise

Show the learner two example messages, one genuine-looking and one fake (make them up, clearly
labelled as examples), and ask which one is a scam and why.
