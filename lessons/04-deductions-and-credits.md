# Lesson 4: Paying less tax, legally
<!-- facts: Medical scheme fees tax credit; Retirement fund contributions; Tax-free savings accounts -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain the difference between a deduction and a credit.
- Say how retirement fund contributions reduce tax, and the limit (from `facts.md`).
- Say how the medical tax credit works.
- Explain what a tax-free savings account is, and the penalty for going over its limits.

## Key ideas

**Deduction vs credit.** A deduction lowers your taxable income, so it saves you your marginal
rate (26% of a deduction saves 26c in the rand at that bracket). A credit comes straight off your
tax, rand for rand.

**Retirement contributions** (pension, provident, retirement annuity) are deductible up to a
percentage of income and a yearly cap (`facts.md`). Someone on a 31% marginal rate who puts R1 000
into a retirement annuity pays about R310 less tax. The trade-off: you can't easily touch that
money before retirement.

**Medical scheme fees tax credit** is a fixed monthly credit for each person on your medical aid
(`facts.md`). Large out-of-pocket medical costs can give extra relief; the rules depend on age and
disability.

**Tax-free savings accounts (TFSA).** You don't pay tax on interest, dividends or growth inside
them. There is an annual and a lifetime limit (`facts.md`), counted across all your TFSAs
together. Going over either one costs a penalty tax on the excess, so track contributions across
providers yourself. Withdrawals don't give you the limit back.

## Exercise

Ask: if Sipho pays R2 000 a month into a retirement annuity and his top bracket is 31%, roughly
how much tax does that save in a year? (R24 000 × 31% ≈ R7 440, assuming he is under the limits.)
